    How to play

    Tap Continue to read on, and choose what Jack does when choices appear. There is no wrong way to read a scene, but there are wrong ways to read Aggie.

    Reading Aggie

    Early on, Aggie says what she thinks. Later she stops volunteering it: about one thing she can’t speak at all, and about the rest she nudges rather than tells. Watch for the moments she changes the subject, answers too briefly, agrees a little too readily, or asks a question instead of giving an answer. Each contract hides one or two things she won’t simply say. When you act on what you think she means, it goes in your journal. Nothing tells you whether you were right. The story will.

    Cover

    Jack’s masked identity draws attention when he refuses work, runs late, or asks too many questions. If the meter fills, Aletheion calls him in. Taking every job keeps it low, at a cost the story remembers.

    You can decline a contract from its card. Side work can be turned down and someone else will fly it; the contracts that matter to Jack’s career can’t, and asking costs cover all the same.

    SNAP plotting

    Each jump is a short puzzle with no timing to it: bring the ship to rest, cast the probe strands, then choose an anchor point with room for the ship and a safety margin, remembering where drifting debris will be when you arrive. A bad anchor costs time, never the game. In Settings you can let Aggie plot the jumps instead.

    Rewinding and saving

    You can rewind to your last choice from the menu. The game saves itself after every step, with one career per mode, and you can copy a save code to carry a career to another browser.

    Spoilers

    With the Spoilers switch off, nothing from Coming of Age is given away: the career ends at Jack’s promotion to tier 4. With it on, the game follows the book to its ending.
